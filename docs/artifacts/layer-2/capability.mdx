<Purpose>

Technology capabilities that can be leveraged to implement security controls.

</Purpose>

<Role>

Capabilities describe what the system *can do*—the attack surface and the
levers available to defenders. Publishing source to a public forge, accepting a
Corporate CLA, or contributing from company infrastructure are capabilities.
Threats attach to them; controls constrain them. Without naming capabilities,
threat models float free of the real platform.

</Role>

<Examples>

<RevealItem title="Public source publication" defaultOpen>

The ability to publish source, configuration or build artefacts to publicly
accessible FOSS platforms. That capability is what makes accidental disclosure
and unreviewed release possible—and what publication controls must govern.

</RevealItem>

<RevealItem title="Contributor agreement acceptance">

The ability to accept CLAs, Corporate CLAs or Developer Certificates of Origin
on behalf of the company. Legal and IP controls exist because this capability
creates lasting obligations.

</RevealItem>

<RevealItem title="Company infrastructure contribution">

The ability to contribute from the company network or managed hardware using
approved tooling. Infrastructure and scanning controls bind to this capability
rather than to “the internet” in general.

</RevealItem>

</Examples>

<AntiPatterns>

<RevealItem title="Capabilities that are really controls">

“Secret scanning” is a control (or a tool). “Public source publication” is the
capability that creates the need for scanning.

</RevealItem>

<RevealItem title="Inventory of products instead of abilities">

Listing GitHub, GitLab and Bitbucket as capabilities without saying what
dangerous or governable ability each affords.

</RevealItem>

</AntiPatterns>
