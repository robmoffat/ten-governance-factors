<GovernanceArtifact
  title="Mapping Document"
  gemaraType="MappingDocument"
  layer="cross"
>

<Purpose>

Declare typed mappings between Gemara entries and external catalogs or
frameworks so coverage and gaps stay explicit.

</Purpose>

<Role>

Programmes rarely invent every obligation from scratch. A mapping document (or
embedded mapping references) shows how local threats, controls or risks relate
to external baselines—FINOS OSR, OSCAL profiles, industry frameworks—without
pretending they are the same object.

</Role>

<Examples>

<RevealItem title="FOSS threats aligned to FINOS OSR risks" defaultOpen>

A FOSS contribution threat catalog extends and imports FINOS Open Source
Readiness risk categories such as data-leakage, legal and reputational risk.
Each local threat can point at the OSR entry it specialises, so assurance
reporting can speak both languages.

</RevealItem>

<RevealItem title="Coverage and no-match gaps">

Mappings record not only matches but `no-match` relationships. That turns “we
think we cover the baseline” into an auditable gap list rather than optimism.

</RevealItem>

</Examples>

<AntiPatterns>

<RevealItem title="Silent equivalence">

Treating an external control id as identical to a local one without a recorded
mapping. Reviewers cannot see what was assumed.

</RevealItem>

<RevealItem title="Mapping without maintenance">

A one-time spreadsheet that never updates when either catalogue versions. Stale
mappings are worse than none—they invent false coverage.

</RevealItem>

</AntiPatterns>
