<GovernanceFactor title="Evidence by Default" number={6}>

<Principle>

Make evidence a normal exhaust of the system, not a special exercise before an
audit.

If posture cannot be explained by attributable receipts, governance is theatre—
no matter how many dashboards you have.

</Principle>

<Problem>

If an audit starts with screenshots, you are already too late. Evidence created
only on request—chat approvals, manual attestation spreadsheets, “evidence
week”—cannot explain posture with receipts. It reconstructs a story after the
fact instead of recording what happened when it happened.

Governed activities and decisions produce facts worth keeping. When those facts
are not emitted automatically into durable, attributable form, the twin has
nothing to show and continuous evaluation has nothing to cite.

</Problem>

<Characteristics>

<RevealItem title="Emitted in the path of work" defaultOpen>

Evidence is produced as the activity or decision runs—not assembled in a scramble
before an auditor arrives.

</RevealItem>

<RevealItem title="Attributable and machine-readable">

Who, what, when and integrity matter. Formats tools can verify beat images and
chat logs that cannot.

</RevealItem>

<RevealItem title="Bound to twins and decisions">

Receipts attach to concrete instances and cite the definition versions and
decision results they support.

</RevealItem>

</Characteristics>

<PositiveExamples>

<RevealItem title="Build provenance emitted automatically">
Release evidence produced as part of the build, not assembled afterwards.
</RevealItem>

<RevealItem title="SBOMs attached to releases">
Composition evidence that travels with the artefact consumers will verify.
</RevealItem>

<RevealItem title="Policy decision logs">
Telemetry of what was asked, which policy applied, and what was decided.
</RevealItem>

<RevealItem title="Signed assessment results">
Control assessment outputs that are attributable and machine-readable.
</RevealItem>

</PositiveExamples>

<NegativeExamples>

<RevealItem title="Screenshot collections">
Unverifiable images that stand in for system-emitted evidence.
</RevealItem>

<RevealItem title="Manual attestation spreadsheets">
Human-typed claims disconnected from identity, time and integrity.
</RevealItem>

<RevealItem title="Approvals that exist only in chat">
Permission granted with no durable, attributable decision record.
</RevealItem>

</NegativeExamples>

<AntiPatterns>

<RevealItem title="Audit theatre">

Producing a pack of artefacts that look compliant without being regenerable from
the system of record.

</RevealItem>

<RevealItem title="Unverifiable claims">

Statements of posture that cannot be checked against provenance, signatures or
decision logs.

</RevealItem>

<RevealItem title="Evidence created only on request">

A scramble that proves evidence was never a normal exhaust of operations.

</RevealItem>

</AntiPatterns>

```mermaid
  Activity->>Policy: Submit facts
  Policy-->>Activity: Decision + explanation
  Activity->>Store: Record evidence automatically
  Note over Store: Provenance · decision log · attestations
  Auditor->>Store: Query evidence later
  Store-->>Auditor: Attributable receipts
```

</Diagram>

<Discussion>

Applying this principle means treating evidence as product of normal operation.

1. **Instrument** sensitive activities and decision boundaries to emit receipts.
2. **Prefer machine-readable, attributable formats**—provenance, SBOMs, decision
   logs, assessment results.
3. **Store evidence against twins** so posture is explained by what accumulated,
   not by memory.
4. **Cite definition versions** so a receipt can say which policy was in force.
5. **Refuse screenshot-first audits** as the primary control story—use them only
   as illustrations of regenerable artefacts.

Supply-chain standards (SLSA, in-toto, CycloneDX) and OSCAL assessment results
show the same pattern: evidence generated as part of the work, not stitched
together later.

</Discussion>
