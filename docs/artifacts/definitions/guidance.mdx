<Purpose>

High-level guidance on cybersecurity measures from industry groups and standards
bodies.

</Purpose>

<Role>

Guidance turns principles into linkable expectations—often adapted from
human-readable policy, frameworks or standards. A guideline is still definition,
not enforcement: it says what should be true (classify information before
publication; register FOSS engagements with the OSPO) so controls and policy can
implement and assess it.

</Role>

<Examples>

<RevealItem title="Information classification before publication" defaultOpen>

FOSS contribution guidance requires contributors to classify material before it
becomes public, so non-public information is not released through a commit or
document. Controls later attach assessment requirements—such as secret scanning
and IP review—to that expectation.

</RevealItem>

<RevealItem title="OSPO programme objective and scope">

Guidelines establish that the company supports certain FOSS contribution
activities when they benefit the company and do not present unacceptable risk,
and they carve out FOSS *consumption* as out of scope. Policy and inventory
controls inherit that boundary.

</RevealItem>

<RevealItem title="AI mitigations upholding AIR principles">

In the FINOS AI Governance Framework, mitigations (guidelines) are written to
uphold catalogued principles such as *Least Exposure* and *Auditability*. The
guidance layer is where “filter before the model sees data” becomes an
expectation controls can implement.

</RevealItem>

</Examples>

<AntiPatterns>

<RevealItem title="Unlinkable prose dumps">

Pasting a PDF policy into a catalogue without stable guideline ids. Nothing
downstream can cite “what was required.”

</RevealItem>

<RevealItem title="Guidance that is secretly policy">

Organisation-specific mandatory rules, thresholds and exception handling belong
in [Policy](/docs/artifacts/definitions/policy). Guidance should stay reusable across
contexts where possible.

</RevealItem>

</AntiPatterns>
