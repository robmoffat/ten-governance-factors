<GovernanceArtifact
  title="Principle"
  gemaraType="PrincipleCatalog"
  layer={1}
>

<Purpose>
Catalog of foundational values that orient guidance and organisational stance.
</Purpose>

<LayerRole>
Layer 1 — definition. Principles sit above technology-specific controls; they explain *why* guidance exists.
</LayerRole>

<WhyYouNeedThis>
Without explicit principles, every guideline becomes an orphan rule. Reviews cannot tell which values a control is protecting, and composition across teams loses a shared north star.
</WhyYouNeedThis>

<Contains>

- **Principle** — `id`, `title`, `description`, optional `group`, `rationale`

</Contains>

<KeyFields>

- Catalog `title` and `metadata` envelope
- Optional `groups[]` for organising principles
- Optional link to a [Lexicon](/docs/artifacts/cross-cutting/lexicon)

</KeyFields>

<LinksUpstream>

- Organisational charters, ethics statements, OSPO mandates

</LinksUpstream>

<LinksDownstream>

- [Guidance](/docs/artifacts/layer-1/guidance) guidelines cite principles (`guidelines[].principles`)
- Informal influence on [Policy](/docs/artifacts/layer-3/policy) scope and risk appetite

</LinksDownstream>

<Example>

Principles for employee FOSS contribution — centralized OSPO ownership, and a hard separation between personal and company activity:

```yaml
principles:
  - id: ospo-governance
    title: Centralized OSPO Governance
    group: governance
    description: >
      The Open Source Program Office establishes, evolves, and executes FOSS
      contribution strategy and governance under direction of the guidelines
      owner.
    rationale: >
      Consistent oversight enables scalable review, inventory management, and
      accountability across engagements.

  - id: separation-personal-company
    title: Separation of Personal and Company Activity
    group: conduct
    description: >
      Personal FOSS contributions must be distinguishable from company
      contributions in identity, infrastructure, representation, and
      intellectual property use.
    rationale: >
      Blurred boundaries create conflict-of-interest, attribution, and IP risk.
```

</Example>

<AntiPatterns>

- Principles that are slogans with no id or description
- Principles restated as if they were enforceable controls
- One giant principle that cannot be cited by guidance

</AntiPatterns>

<Discussion>

Principles are cheap to author and expensive to violate. In Gemara they remain Layer 1: they do not evaluate builds or block merges by themselves. They give guidance and later audits a stable vocabulary of intent.

</Discussion>
